# Holo-Org Failure Cases Simulation

## Purpose
Deliberately break the Holo-Org funnel before real traffic does. This hardens the pricing page, catalog, start flow, Role Analyzer, enterprise page, BYO model, and governance modules.

Source inputs:
- Commercial launch matrix CSV
- Pricing page/catalog output
- First 10 customers simulation
- Corrected commercial model: HoloOrg Patterns free/open; Holo-Org Commercial paid platform/deployment/governance/transformation/managed ops

---

# Executive Summary

The most dangerous failures are not technical. They are commercial trust failures.

The site fails if:
1. The visitor cannot tell where to start within 30 seconds.
2. Enterprise buyers see cheap SaaS framing around the 10,000-agent offer.
3. Governance, consent, audit, and evidence are hidden as features instead of priced layers.
4. BYO is not explicit enough for enterprise buyers.
5. Open/free HoloOrg Patterns blur into paid services without boundary.
6. CTAs lead to empty drawers, `#`, vague pages, or disconnected flows.
7. Role Analyzer is not the dominant path for uncertain buyers.

---

# Failure Case 1 — Visitor lands and cannot tell where to start

## Scenario
A visitor lands on the homepage and sees AI workforce, agents, patterns, marketplace, products, and enterprise language all mixed together.

## Failure signal
They ask: “Is this for me?” and leave.

## Root cause
Too many concepts before routing.

## Required fix
Every homepage and pricing hero must offer a clear start block:

- I want to test a small AI workflow → Starter Pack
- I want to improve a team workflow → Small Team Pack
- I do not know what fits → Role Analyzer
- I want a governed pilot → 100-Agent Pilot
- I am enterprise/government → Enterprise Architecture Session

## Acceptance test
A first-time visitor can choose a path in under 30 seconds.

# Failure Case 2 — Role Analyzer is buried

## Scenario
A customer is interested but not sure what to buy.

## Failure signal
They browse product catalog cards and leave because nothing feels exactly right.

## Root cause
The diagnostic wedge is not obvious enough.

## Required fix
Role Analyzer must appear:
- homepage hero secondary CTA
- pricing page top block
- product catalog top block
- start-here flow
- enterprise page as architecture entry

## Acceptance test
Every uncertain route points to Role Analyzer.

# Failure Case 3 — 10,000-agent holarchy looks like a SaaS tier

## Scenario
Enterprise/government buyer sees the 10,000-agent offer in a normal price card beside starter packs.

## Failure signal
They dismiss it as hype or unserious.

## Root cause
Wrong visual and commercial framing.

## Required fix
10,000-agent holarchy must be presented as:
- enterprise architecture program
- procurement-grade transformation
- risk/governance model
- private/sovereign deployment option
- multi-phase implementation

## Forbidden
- Buy Now CTA
- cheap pricing card
- simple monthly-only framing
- vague “Contact us” without procurement language

## Acceptance test
The offer feels like a board/procurement-level program, not a SaaS subscription.

---

# Failure Case 4 — Free patterns confuse paid product boundary

## Scenario
A technically minded visitor sees HoloOrg Patterns are free/open and assumes everything should be free.

## Failure signal
They download/read patterns but do not understand what is paid.

## Root cause
Open architecture and paid implementation are not separated.

## Required fix
Use this split everywhere:

Free:
- reference architecture
- pattern language
- conceptual diagrams
- starting guides

Paid:
- diagnostics
- implementation
- deployment
- runtime
- governance
- integrations
- managed operations
- enterprise transformation

## Acceptance test
Visitor can explain the difference between HoloOrg Patterns and Holo-Org Commercial.

# Failure Case 7 — Cheap products weaken enterprise credibility

## Scenario
Enterprise buyer sees $29 packs and $5M transformation in the same visual rhythm.

## Failure signal
The high-end offer feels inflated or ridiculous.

## Root cause
No visual separation between entry and transformation.

## Required fix
Split pricing into two zones:

Zone A: Start Small
- Starter Packs
- Small Team Packs
- Diagnostics

Zone B: Build an AI Workforce
- 100-Agent Pilot
- 1,000-Agent Transformation
- 10,000-Agent Holarchy

## Acceptance test
Enterprise path does not feel contaminated by low-ticket ecommerce styling.

# Failure Case 8 — Small buyers are forced into sales calls

## Scenario

## Failure signal

# Failure Case 9 — Mid buyers cannot see the 100-agent pilot as a concrete offer

## Scenario
Department leader wants a serious but bounded starting point.

## Failure signal
They see either tiny packs or huge enterprise transformation.

## Root cause
100-Agent Pilot is not positioned as the bridge.

## Required fix
100-Agent Pilot must be the main Mid-market bridge:
- 4-8 weeks
- one function or department
- role mapping
- governance model
- pilot dashboard
- evidence reporting

# Failure Case 10 — Dead CTAs destroy trust

## Scenario
Visitor clicks Explore, Deploy, Product, or Request Early Access and hits `#`, empty drawer, or broken route.

## Failure signal
Trust drops immediately.

## Root cause
CTA route discipline is missing.

## Required fix
Allowed CTA destinations only:
- View Patterns
- Run Role Analyzer
- Buy Starter Pack
- Start Team Package
- Book Design Session
- Talk to Sales
- Request Enterprise Proposal
- Discuss BYO Deployment
- Add Governance Layer

## Acceptance test
No CTA routes to `#`, empty product drawers, undefined states, or generic non-converting pages.

---

# Failure Case 11 — Case study claims trigger credibility/legal risk

## Scenario
Site publishes deployment claims without evidence or permission.

## Failure signal
Buyer asks for proof; claim cannot be defended.

## Root cause
Reality Ledger classification missing.

## Required fix
Every major deployment claim gets classified:
- REAL: evidence and permission exist
- PARTIAL: internal/related but not public-proof ready
- PRETEND/internal only: do not publish externally

## Acceptance test
No public claim appears without evidence status.

# Failure Case 14 — Buyer routes are not role-specific

## Scenario
Business, technical, and governance buyers all see the same generic pitch.

## Failure signal
Each stakeholder thinks it is aimed at someone else.

## Root cause
No buyer-route split.

## Required fix
Create three routes:

Business:
- ROI
- workforce design
- operating model
- decision rights

Technical:
- runtime
- integration
- deployment
- telemetry
- architecture

Governance:
- consent
- audit
- compliance
- evidence
- escalation

## Acceptance test
Each buyer can enter through their own route and still converge on the same commercial ladder.

# Failure Case 15 — Pricing lacks reason for jumps

## Scenario
Customer sees $499, then $75k, then $750k, then $5M.

## Failure signal
The ladder looks arbitrary.

## Root cause
Pricing not tied to scale, risk, and deployment maturity.

## Required fix
Explain price jumps using:
- agent count
- workflow count
- decision risk
- integrations
- governance depth
- deployment environment
- support/ops layer
- procurement burden

## Acceptance test
Buyer understands why 100-agent pilot, 1,000-agent transformation, and 10,000-agent holarchy cost radically different amounts.

# Failure Case 16 — Managed operations is not attached

## Scenario
Customer buys setup but no ongoing managed layer.

## Failure signal

## Root cause
Managed operations not packaged.

## Required fix

- monitoring
- optimisation

- incident response

# Failure Case 17 — Site does not support partner/channel sales

## Scenario
Partner or reseller wants to understand what they can sell.

## Failure signal
They cannot tell whether patterns, packs, or pilots are partner-ready.

## Root cause
No channel version of offers.

## Required fix
Add partner/channel flags internally:
- self-serve
- assisted
- partner-sellable
- procurement-only
- white-label candidate

## Acceptance test
Offer matrix supports direct, partner, outbound, and procurement motions.

---

# Failure Case 18 — BYO creates support blowout

## Scenario
Customer brings messy tools and expects Holo-Org to own every failure.

## Failure signal
Margin collapses through support.

## Root cause
BYO support boundaries not written.

## Required fix
BYO page must define responsibility split:
- customer owns model/cloud/data/platform billing and uptime
- Holo-Org owns orchestration design, governance, reporting, and agreed integrations
- premium support required for complex BYO

## Acceptance test
BYO increases trust without creating unlimited liability.

---

# Failure Case 19 — Governance modules are optional when they should be required

## Scenario
Regulated customer skips governance to save money.

## Failure signal
Risk increases and offer becomes unsafe.

## Root cause
No regulated-use rule.

## Required fix
For healthcare, finance, government, education, child/family, BCI/neural, and sensitive data use cases:
- governance module is required or strongly defaulted
- not shown as cosmetic add-on

## Acceptance test
Regulated buyers are routed through governance by default.

# Final instruction to Pen

Do not ship a beautiful page that fails routing.

The site is ready only when:
- uncertain buyers go to Role Analyzer
- small buyers can buy/start without enterprise friction
- mid buyers see the 100-agent pilot
- enterprise buyers see architecture/procurement weight
- regulated buyers see governance immediately
- BYO buyers see they can keep their stack
- every CTA resolves
- every major claim has evidence status

If any of these fail, block launch.
