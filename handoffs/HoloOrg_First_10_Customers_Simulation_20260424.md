# Holo-Org First 10 Customers Simulation

## Purpose
Pressure-test the Holo-Org pricing ladder, product placement, CTA routing, and sales motion before real traffic hits.

This simulation uses the corrected commercial model:
- HoloOrg Patterns = open/free reference architecture
- Holo-Org Commercial = paid diagnostics, packs, pilots, platform, deployment, governance, transformation, and managed ops

## Simulation result summary

The first 10 customers should not all be forced into one pricing table. They should distribute across entry, small, pilot, mid, and enterprise pathways.

Expected revenue from first 10 customers:
- Low case: ~$41k first-year value
- Realistic case: ~$355k first-year value
- Strong case: ~$1.2M+ first-year value

Main conclusion:
The money is not in the first cheap packs. The money is in using them to create diagnostics, proof, pilots, and transformation pathways.

# Flow failures found by simulation

## Failure 1 — cheap entry may weaken enterprise seriousness
Fix: visually separate Starter Packs from Transformation.

## Failure 2 — Role Analyzer must be unavoidable
Fix: every uncertain buyer route should point to Role Analyzer.

## Failure 3 — governance must not be buried
Fix: surface ConsentX, LifeGraph, audit, approval, and evidence on pricing and enterprise pages.

## Failure 4 — BYO must be a formal branch
Fix: managed vs BYO toggle on pricing and enterprise pages.

## Failure 5 — enterprise offer cannot use normal checkout styling
Fix: procurement-style CTA and architecture-program language.

## Failure 6 — all CTAs must resolve
Fix: no `#`, no empty drawers, no vague “Explore” links.
