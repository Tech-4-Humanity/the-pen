# Canonical Glossary

## Adaptation

A candidate organisational response to pressure, expressed through changed behaviour, structure, technology, policy, relationship or operating practice.

## Augmentation

The use of intelligent tools to increase the productivity or range of an existing human, role, team or process without changing the organisation's underlying atomic unit.

## Capability

A governed, repeatable and reusable organisational ability to produce a defined outcome, independent of the specific human, agent, system or partner providing it at a given time.

## Capability Network

A connected set of capabilities that can be composed around an outcome across organisational and provider boundaries.

## Canon

The governed and evolving body of theory, frameworks, research, evidence, stories, software, implementation doctrine, failures and lessons associated with organisational evolution.

## Evolution

A durable change in the operating characteristics of an organisation produced by accumulated selected adaptations, capabilities, learning and memory.

## Flourishing

The normative test of whether organisational evolution increases durable capability, dignity, resilience and health for people and the wider systems the organisation inhabits.

## Institutional Memory

Durable, governed retention of context, evidence, provenance, decisions, relationships, outcomes and lessons so the organisation can learn across time rather than only through individual recollection.

## Living Organisation

An organisation able to sense, remember, learn, adapt, recover and reorganise while preserving explicit purpose, human dignity, authority and accountability.

## OIKOS

The living organisation model: an interconnected organisational habitat of purpose, identity, people, relationships, knowledge, memory, capabilities, assets, authority, evidence, outcomes, learning, stewardship and ecosystem relationships.

## Organisational Evolution Framework

The observable progression from hierarchy through augmentation and capability networks toward living organisations and regenerative ecosystems.

## Organisational Evolution Theory

The causal explanation of why and how organisations evolve through purpose, pressure, adaptation, selection, capability, learning, memory, evolution and flourishing.

## Pressure

A condition that makes the current organisational state insufficient, including market, technological, regulatory, social, ecological, competitive, crisis or internal-failure forces.

## Provider

A human, AI agent, software service, robot, external partner, community participant or public institution that supplies a capability.

## Regenerative Ecosystem

A system in which organisations do more than reduce harm and instead increase the capability, resilience and health of employees, customers, suppliers, communities, institutions and natural systems.

## Runtime

The governed execution substrate supporting identity, authority, planning, provider invocation, workflows, evidence, receipts, telemetry, recovery and lifecycle. Runtime enables parts of the living organisation but does not define its purpose.

## Selection

The process through which evidence, governance, leadership, culture, regulation, markets and outcomes determine which adaptations are retained, changed or extinguished.

## Trust With Evidence

A governance condition in which action is enabled through explicit identity and authority and remains inspectable through provenance, receipts, outcomes, telemetry and recovery.
