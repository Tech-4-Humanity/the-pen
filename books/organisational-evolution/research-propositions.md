# Research Propositions and Evidence Agenda

## Claim-state discipline

Every substantial claim should be classified as one of:

- **Observed** — supported by existing evidence or documented cases
- **Theoretical** — reasoned from established concepts or the model
- **Hypothesis** — testable but not yet established
- **Normative** — describes what ought to occur
- **Aspirational** — describes a desired possible future

## Core propositions

### P1 — Capability reuse and coordination cost

Organisations with higher reuse of governed capabilities will require less coordination effort for repeated outcome classes than organisations that repeatedly assemble work through role and departmental routing.

Potential measures:

- coordination hours per outcome
- hand-offs per outcome
- elapsed cycle time
- capability reuse frequency
- exception rate

### P2 — Institutional memory and repeated failure

Organisations with durable evidence-linked institutional memory will repeat materially similar operational failures less frequently than organisations that retain lessons primarily through individual memory or disconnected documentation.

Potential measures:

- recurrence rate of known failure classes
- evidence retrieval success
- time to identify prior precedent
- staff turnover sensitivity
- recovery time

### P3 — Evidence-backed authority and trust

Explicit identity, authority and evidence capture will increase justified organisational trust while reducing dependence on positional authority alone.

Potential measures:

- authority traceability
- audit completion time
- decision contestability
- reversal rate
- stakeholder trust measures

### P4 — Capability-centric adaptation speed

Capability-centric organisations will adapt faster to material environmental pressure than role-centric organisations, provided governance and provider substitution are mature.

Potential measures:

- time from pressure detection to deployed response
- number of structural approvals required
- provider substitution time
- capability recomposition rate
- outcome stability during change

### P5 — Learning from execution and resilience

Organisations that connect execution evidence, telemetry, recovery and learning will recover faster and improve more reliably after failure than organisations that treat incident resolution as an isolated operational event.

Potential measures:

- mean time to recovery
- mean time to learning adoption
- recurrence after remediation
- proportion of incidents producing reusable learning
- policy or capability changes derived from incidents

### P6 — Augmentation without redesign

AI augmentation applied without organisational redesign will initially increase local productivity but may not improve end-to-end outcomes where approvals, incentives, silos and knowledge loss remain unchanged.

Potential measures:

- local task time versus end-to-end cycle time
- output volume versus outcome quality
- queue growth downstream
- approval latency
- rework rate

### P7 — Provider independence and organisational durability

Capabilities specified independently from their providers will be more durable and adaptable than capabilities embedded in a single role, vendor or technology.

Potential measures:

- provider replacement time
- continuity during provider loss
- portability of policies and evidence rules
- dependency concentration
- capability survival across reorganisations

### P8 — Regenerative performance

Organisations that increase stakeholder and ecosystem capability will demonstrate stronger long-term resilience than organisations optimised solely for short-term extraction, subject to sector and environmental conditions.

Potential measures:

- employee capability growth
- customer dependency versus capability growth
- supplier resilience
- community value contribution
- environmental restoration indicators
- long-horizon organisational survival

## Required comparison sets

- organisations that adapted successfully
- organisations that failed to adapt
- organisations that adapted but caused ecosystem harm
- hierarchical organisations that remain effective under stable or high-control conditions
- capability-network experiments that failed
- public-sector and regulated-sector cases

## Research programme

1. Literature map and prior-theory alignment
2. Canonical construct definitions
3. Diagnostic instrument development
4. Capability and memory metrics
5. Cross-sectional case studies
6. Longitudinal organisational studies
7. Runtime reference implementations
8. Counterexample and failure-mode catalogue
9. Flourishing index development
10. Independent replication and critique
