# Augmented Memory Program Overview

## Build Position

Augmented Memory is a product family and infrastructure layer for capturing, structuring, revisiting, sharing, and discovering meaningful human stories across families, organisations, learning environments, care settings, and public places.

The platform is not a single archive. It is a reusable memory graph with front-end products for Life, Legacy, Work, Care, Learning, and Places.

## Category Definition

Augmented Memory creates a world where:

- people can capture life as it happens
- older memories can be enriched where evidence exists
- future memories are captured with less effort
- memories can attach to people, events, places, objects, organisations, and rituals
- public places can carry stories without needing a physical marker everywhere
- physical triggers such as QR, NFC, plaques, printed cards, and venue markers can be added where they improve experience
- privacy, consent, visibility, archive, and deletion are first-class controls

## Strategic Doctrine

Preserve what was real. Surface it at the right moment. Let people choose how visible it becomes.

Do not position the product as imitation, fantasy continuity, or a replacement for real human connection. The product preserves authentic stories, voice, media, context, values, and place-based meaning.

## Core Product Families

1. Augmented Memories Life: living families and individuals.
2. Augmented Memories Legacy: remembrance, memorials, family tribute pages, cemetery-linked stories, and continuity of family history.
3. Augmented Memories Work: decisions, client context, knowledge continuity.
4. Augmented Memories Care: ageing, disability, identity continuity, caregiver support.
5. Augmented Memories Learning: student progress, reading, confidence, teacher/parent continuity.
6. Augmented Memories Places: public/place memory, travel, venues, tourism, museums, heritage, ambient discovery.

## Build Priority

MVP1 must prioritise Life, Legacy, and Places because these create the emotional wedge, public differentiation, and partner pathway.

MVP2 adds Work, Care, and Learning because these are deeper operating environments requiring more integration, governance, and support.

MVP3 expands into advanced experience layers such as emotional memory, guided reflection, curated public networks, city trails, museums, and richer ambient discovery.

## Non-Negotiables

- Device-agnostic ingestion.
- Private by default.
- Public sharing must be optional.
- Users own what they upload.
- Families can disagree, but the platform enforces content ownership and visibility settings.
- Legacy records must use careful archive and retention rules.
- Public memory must be moderated and ranked to avoid spam.
- Physical markers are optional anchors, not platform dependency.
- Place graph starts virtual-first and can become physically anchored later.
