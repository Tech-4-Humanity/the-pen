Quick screening logic

Go provisional: novel, strategically core, disclosure risk rising, conversion budget likely.
Delay: concept exists, but data, inventorship or ownership is still blurry.
Do not file: no product path, no budget, weak novelty, or better kept as a trade secret.

Trade secret steps

1. Identify the secret precisely, what it is, where it lives, who owns it, and why it creates economic value.
2. Mark its custody, repositories, access permissions, and every person or vendor with exposure.
3. Record secrecy controls: NDAs, employment clauses, contractor terms, least-privilege access, logs, watermarking, segmented storage.
4. Maintain version history and evidence of continuous control so the secret can survive discovery and misappropriation disputes.
5. Prepare litigation-safe summaries that prove existence and control without disclosing the secret itself unless compelled.

Common law vs registered trademarks

Common law rightsRegistered rights
Arise from actual market use.Arise from formal registration.
Usually narrower geographically and harder to prove.Usually clearer, stronger, and easier to enforce.
Depend on evidence of first use, continuity, and market recognition.Depend on prosecution quality, renewal, and class coverage.
Useful as an interim shield.Better for scaling, fundraising, licensing and disputes.

Mark cleanup rules

Keep one canonical brand register with mark, owner, classes, territories, first-use date, filing status, and linked product.
Retire duplicate or abandoned names fast; they create false asset inflation.
For every active product name, choose one state only: registered, filed, common-law only, or candidate not yet used.
