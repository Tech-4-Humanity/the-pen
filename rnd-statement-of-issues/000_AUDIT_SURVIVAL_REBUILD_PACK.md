## TI-01 — exemplar investigation
Title: Adaptive AI Suitability Classification: AI Sweet Spots / Black Spots / Other Spots.

Problem statement:
Generative AI was increasingly deployed on the broad assumption that more AI use improves productivity, communication, participation, and efficiency. The company observed inconsistent outcomes: some users experienced uplift, while others experienced overload, dependency, confusion, workflow fragmentation, or organisational drag.

Technical uncertainty:
Could AI suitability be classified by person, task, role, cognitive context, communication style, environment, team, and support context, rather than treated as universal productivity uplift?

Hypothesis:
AI benefit is not universal. A useful adaptive system must classify not only where AI helps, but also where it harms, overloads, creates dependency, slows a team, degrades participation, or should step back.

Method:
Iterative applied experimentation across AI-assisted workflows, communication tasks, role/team structures, support environments, prompting approaches, differing cognitive/communication contexts, and participation scenarios.

Observed variables:
- uplift;
- overload;
- confusion;
- communication friction;
- repeated clarification;
- dependency;
- organisational drag;
- workflow fragmentation;
- participation changes;
- context-specific outcomes.

Hypothesis evolution:
Sweet Spots originally meant where AI helped. The model evolved because negative and mixed states were equally important. Black Spots and Other Spots emerged as classification extensions.

New knowledge:
AI suitability is a contextual classification problem, not a generic productivity assumption.

Evidence to harvest:
- AI Sweet Spots source artefacts;
- Black Spots notes;
- Other Spots notes;
- suitability classification diagrams/tables;
- productivity/role/team mapping;
- NDIS / Outcome Ready links;
- WorkFamilyAI links;
- MyNeuralSignal links;
- GitHub/Drive/Notion/Canva/docs/screenshots/timestamps.
