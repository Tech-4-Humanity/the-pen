# Doolittles / Dr Doolittle — Talk to the Animals

## Working name
Doolittles

## Reference line
Talk to the animals.

## Product thesis
Every organisation is full of animals: Slack, SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Microsoft, Jira, payroll, LMS, HRIS, finance, procurement, security, and hundreds of agent workers. They all make noise, but they do not hear the same thing. Doolittles translates one executive announcement into the language each human, tool, system, workflow, and agent pod needs — then starts the right preparation immediately.

## Category
Enterprise Command Translation and Tool Activation Layer.

## Core promise
Say it once. Every human hears it properly. Every system starts preparing.

---

## Why now
Organisations already own the tools. The bottleneck is not software availability. The bottleneck is translation latency:

- leaders say something once;
- teams interpret it differently;
- tools find out late;
- documentation is written after the fact;
- training is created after confusion appears;
- service desks learn from tickets rather than from the original command;
- neurodiverse staff carry extra ambiguity and social-translation load.

Doolittles removes that delay by giving tools the same early warning as people — but in machine-readable language.

---

## Simple story
The leader says: "We are standardising on Microsoft next month."

Traditional organisation:

- procurement asks what to buy;
- IT waits for scope;
- HR waits for training detail;
- support finds out near launch;
- documentation is late;

Doolittles organisation:
- Slack receives team-specific updates;
- SAP receives procurement and finance preparation tasks;
- ServiceNow receives support categories, macros, and change tickets;
- Salesforce receives account and customer-impact messaging;
- LMS receives training modules;
- Microsoft admin receives tenant/IAM setup plans;
- documentation is drafted on the fly;
- neurodiverse workers receive concrete checklists in their preferred format;
- the readiness dashboard updates immediately.

## Narrative vignette — Dr Doolittle enters the boardroom
The boardroom announcement is simple.

"Fuel prices are rising. Reduce travel and prepare lower-cost operations."

In the old world, the sentence leaves the room as a vague email. Everyone hears a different animal sound. Finance hears cost. HR hears policy. Operations hears routes. IT hears systems. Salesforce hears customers. SAP hears vendors. ServiceNow hears tickets. Slack hears noise.

Dr Doolittle hears the zoo.

He translates once.

Slack gets team-ready messages. SAP gets cost and procurement instructions. ServiceNow gets change and support tasks. Salesforce gets customer impact. HR gets staff instructions. Neurodiverse workers get concrete steps and no implied meaning. The tools do not wait for next week. They start preparing now.

By the time the first meeting would have started, the organisation already has a readiness board.

## Stats to use in narrative
These are positioning metrics for demo modelling, not external claims until independently sourced per campaign.

- Traditional enterprise change often experiences weeks of interpretation and coordination delay.
- Target demo compression: 6-8 weeks down to 24-72 hours for initial readiness.
- Target operating benefit: 20-40% less coordination waste in change preparation.
- Target support benefit: fewer avoidable tickets because support and documentation are prepared before rollout.
- Target neuroinclusive benefit: reduced ambiguity by converting announcements into explicit, channel-matched instructions.
