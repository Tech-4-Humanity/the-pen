# Act I Editorial Review

## Scope

Chapters 1–6 of the canonical rewrite.

## Result

Act I now functions as a continuous techno-thriller opening rather than a chain of speculative vignettes.

## Strengths

- The forgotten airport session remains a credible inciting incident.
- Alex is active: he documents, reconstructs access, sets a trap and challenges Maya.
- Rachel provides a disciplined distinction between evidence and interpretation without dismissing Alex.
- Maya has competence, secrecy, history and an immediate cost to trust.
- The 380-person observational cohort is now integral to the plot rather than decorative research context.
- The continuity extension gives the watcher plausible access without magical hacking.
- Each chapter changes the evidence state and ends with altered stakes.

## Risks requiring later revision

- Technical mechanics of browser policy installation and offline command execution require a specialist plausibility pass.
- Alex and Rachel need additional personal history so their relationship does not remain purely functional.
- Maya's connection to Elias Vale and her missing brother must be disclosed with a chronology that survives scrutiny.
- The anonymous actor's ability to anticipate the decoy environment must receive a grounded explanation.
- Chapter lengths remain short for a commercial novel and may need expansion after the full plot is stable.
- The prose still uses occasional short-line emphasis inherited from the original style; this should be reduced during copyediting.
