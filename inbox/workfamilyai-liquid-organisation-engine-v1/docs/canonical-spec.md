# Liquid Organisation Engine — Canonical Specification v1.0

## 1. Position in the Canon

The engine is an application of the Organisational Evolution Framework and WorkFamilyAI runtime. It does not replace the Theory, OIKOS or Runtime Kernel.

Theory explains why evolution occurs.
Framework describes states and transitions.
OIKOS describes the living organisation.
Runtime executes governed capabilities.
The Liquid Organisation Engine asks questions, runs scenarios, predicts likely operating consequences and recommends evidence-backed adaptations.

## 2. Unit of analysis

Primary unit: one role record within the 10x10x10 organisational map.

Each record binds:
- leader/pillar
- function
- role
- required and produced artefacts
- internal and external dependencies
- systems and authority
- operating tempo
- KPIs and service expectations
- onboarding and offboarding
- human contribution and accountability
- AI augmentation pattern
- evidence and telemetry
- future-state pathway

Organisation-level predictions aggregate role-level records without erasing local variation.

## 3. Ten predictive variables

1. Purpose Alignment — clarity, legitimacy and connection between role activity and intended outcomes.
2. Pressure Load — demand, volatility, urgency, complexity, regulation and environmental stress.
3. Human Capacity — skills, cognition, wellbeing, judgement, relationships and available attention.
4. AI Fit — suitability of AI for the work, including task structure, ambiguity, data quality and reversibility.
5. Authority Clarity — who may decide, approve, override, appeal and remain accountable.
6. Artefact Integrity — completeness, freshness, provenance, accessibility and consistency of required documents and data.
7. Flow and Tempo — queue age, handoffs, cycle time, interruptions, synchronisation and response speed.
8. Dependency Resilience — concentration, bottlenecks, substitutes, interoperability and recovery paths.
9. Learning and Memory — feedback quality, experimentation, selection, knowledge retention and reuse.
10. Trust and Flourishing — safety, fairness, inclusion, confidence, meaningful work, stakeholder value and ecological/social effects.

## 4. Sweet spots and blackspots

An AI Sweet Spot occurs where AI Fit is strong, Artefact Integrity and Authority Clarity are adequate, human capacity is amplified, outcomes improve and harm remains reversible and observable.

An AI Blackspot occurs where one or more of the following are present:
- high consequence with weak authority or appeal
- poor, stale or biased evidence
- hidden workload displacement
- automation of relational, ethical or contextual judgement without safeguards
- speed exceeding governance or human comprehension
- dependency concentration without recovery
- metric improvement that reduces trust, dignity or flourishing
- confident output with low explainability or low source quality

Blackspots are not merely low-scoring sweet spots. They are risk patterns requiring containment, redesign or prohibition.

## 5. Prediction outputs

For every role, team, pillar and organisation, the engine may produce:
- current evolutionary-state mix
- sweet-spot index
- blackspot risk
- operational friction score
- human load and capacity risk
- artefact readiness
- authority and governance readiness
- dependency fragility
- learning velocity
- likely response to specified pressure
- predicted role evolution
- recommended intervention
- expected benefits and harms
- evidence confidence
- unknowns and questions still required

Exact forecasts must be labelled calibrated, directional or hypothetical. The system must never represent unsupported coefficients as scientific fact.

## 6. Interactive assessment

Question paths adapt to prior answers and observed data. They operate at four levels:

### Organisation
Purpose, pressures, operating state, constraints, values, strategic outcomes.

### Pillar/team
Demand, service levels, cross-functional dependencies, systems, risks and capability gaps.

### Role
Artefacts, decisions, tempo, pain points, human strengths, AI use, exceptions and escalation.

### Scenario
A concrete change or shock and the likely response across roles and capabilities.

The interface must show why each question matters and what evidence could answer it automatically.

## 7. Scenario engine

Required scenarios include:
- demand doubles in 30 days
- key person leaves
- new employee onboarding wave
- restructure or merger
- regulatory change
- major system outage
- cyber or data incident
- budget reduction
- rapid growth
- launch of new product or geography
- AI adoption increase
- inaccurate AI output enters a critical process
- trust collapse or employee resistance
- supplier or partner failure
- high-performing neurodivergent team requiring different work patterns

Users can modify pressure, duration, affected roles and constraints. The engine returns first-order effects, cascading effects, time horizon, affected artefacts, required decisions, human impacts, mitigations and evidence requirements.

## 8. Evolutionary-state model

The engine uses a mixed-state profile rather than a single maturity label:
- Hierarchy
- Augmentation
- Capability Networks
- Living Organisation
- Regenerative Ecosystem

Each organisation, pillar or role can hold proportions across states. Scenario results may show progression or regression.

## 9. Human constitutional constraints

All recommendations must preserve:
- human purpose and dignity
- legitimate authority and accountability
- right to understand, challenge and appeal
- meaningful work and learning
- inclusion and cognitive diversity
- transparent evidence and confidence
- recovery and human override

No recommendation may use headcount elimination as its default success measure. Capacity released by automation must be linked to improved outcomes, safety, learning, service, innovation, resilience or wellbeing.

## 10. Measurement model

Baseline and response measurements include:
- outcome attainment
- cycle and wait time
- quality/error/rework
- stakeholder response
- employee load and wellbeing
- adoption and trust
- exception and override frequency
- evidence completeness
- learning retained and reused
- recovery performance
- financial/resource impact
- externality and flourishing measures

Every predicted intervention must define what will prove or disprove it.

## 11. Technical requirements

The deployable application requires:
- role and artefact search
- interactive assessment wizard
- scenario builder
- variable sliders with defaults and provenance
- dependency graph
- heatmaps across 1,000 roles
- sweet-spot/blackspot views
- role detail and future-forward narrative
- comparison of baseline and scenarios
- saved assessments and version history
- evidence links and confidence labels
- export to JSON, CSV, XLSX and PDF
- receipt generation for every assessment and scenario run

## 12. Initial prediction method

Version 1 begins with transparent rules and weighted scoring, not opaque machine learning.

Each variable is scored 0–100 with:
- observed evidence
- user responses
- inherited role defaults
- confidence
- freshness

Predictions use documented rules and dependency propagation. Telemetry and validated outcomes later calibrate weights. Machine learning may be added only when training data, target definitions, bias controls and replayable validation exist.

## 13. Definition of done

The capability is REAL only when:
- the app is deployed
- all 1,000 role records are queryable
- assessment questions work
- scenarios can be executed and compared
- prediction reasoning is visible
- saved runs emit receipts
- telemetry records user response and actual outcomes
- at least one real organisation completes baseline and follow-up measurement
