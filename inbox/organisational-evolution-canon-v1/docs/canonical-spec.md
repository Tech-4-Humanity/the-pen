# Canonical Specification v1.0

## 1. Scope

This specification freezes the first deployable expression of the Organisational Evolution body of work. It separates explanation, application and implementation so that technical infrastructure does not displace the human and institutional purpose.

## 2. Naming hierarchy

### Organisational Evolution Theory

The explanatory model. It answers: **Why do organisations evolve?**

### Organisational Evolution Framework

The practical reference model. It answers: **How can the stages and transitions be understood and assessed?**

### Organisational Evolution Canon

The complete body of philosophy, theories, frameworks, evidence, stories, methods, implementation doctrine, applications and learning.

## 3. The evolutionary engine

The canonical sequence is:

1. **Purpose** — the reason the system exists and the boundary for legitimate change.
2. **Pressure** — environmental, social, economic, technological, regulatory or ecological forces that make the current state insufficient.
3. **Adaptation** — candidate behaviours, structures, technologies and relationships developed in response.
4. **Selection** — evidence, governance, culture, markets and lived outcomes determine which adaptations should persist.
5. **Capability** — selected adaptations become repeatable, owned and reusable capacities.
6. **Learning** — outcomes and failures are interpreted and converted into improved practice.
7. **Memory** — learning becomes durable institutional knowledge with provenance and context.
8. **Evolution** — accumulated capability and memory change the organisation's operating characteristics.
9. **Flourishing** — evolution creates durable value for people and the wider systems the organisation inhabits.

The engine is cyclical. Flourishing changes purpose and creates new pressures; failure may trigger recovery, reselection or extinction of low-value adaptations.

## 4. Ecosystem participants

The theory applies across and between:

- People
- Artificial intelligence
- Organisations
- Communities
- Governments
- Nature

These are participants and affected systems, not interchangeable moral entities. Human authority, rights, dignity and accountability remain explicit.

## 5. Evolutionary states

| State | Organised around | Intelligence resides in | Primary objective | Leadership mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy | People, roles and departments | Individuals | Efficiency | Direct and control |
| Augmentation | People and AI | Human–AI teams | Productivity | Orchestrate |
| Capability Networks | Reusable capabilities | Organisation-wide systems | Adaptability | Design systems |
| Living Organisations | Organisational intelligence | OIKOS: relationships, memory, evidence and learning | Continuous learning | Cultivate |
| Regenerative Ecosystems | Collective intelligence | Interconnected institutions and environments | Flourishing | Steward |

The states are not a mandatory linear maturity ladder. Organisations may contain multiple states at once and may regress under pressure.

## 6. OIKOS

OIKOS is the Living Organisation Model. It describes the organisation as an interconnected system of:

- Purpose and identity
- People and relationships
- Knowledge and memory
- Capabilities and assets
- Decisions and authority
- Culture and governance
- Evidence and outcomes
- Learning and stewardship
- Ecosystem relationships

OIKOS is not merely a knowledge system and is not the runtime engine.

## 7. Runtime Kernel

The Runtime Kernel is implementation doctrine beneath OIKOS. It supports safe execution through:

- Identity
- Authority
- Capability discovery
- Planning
- Providers
- Execution
- Evidence and receipts
- Telemetry
- Recovery
- Lifecycle and learning

Runtime makes parts of the model executable. It does not define the philosophy or destination.

## 8. The Great Inversion

| Scarcity-era assumption | Abundant-intelligence orientation |
|---|---|
| Departments | Capabilities |
| Jobs | Outcomes and contributions |
| Managers | Orchestrators and stewards |
| Static hierarchy | Dynamic networks |
| Information flow | Governed intelligence flow |
| Control | Evidence-backed trust |
| Optimisation | Adaptation |
| Efficiency alone | Flourishing and regeneration |

## 9. Invariants

Across every state, the following remain constitutional:

- Human purpose and dignity
- Ethics and legitimate authority
- Trust and accountability
- Relationships and inclusion
- Creativity and curiosity
- Learning and contestability
- Stewardship across generations
- Evidence before claims

## 10. Claim discipline

Directional changes may be illustrated. Exact productivity, cost, decision-speed or value multipliers must be marked as hypotheses until supported by named evidence, transparent measurement and reproducible methods.

## 11. Canon structure

1. Augmented Humanity — philosophy and desired future.
2. Organisational Evolution Theory — explanatory mechanism.
3. Organisational Evolution Framework — states, transitions and assessment.
4. OIKOS — living organisation model.
5. Runtime Kernel — execution doctrine.
6. Experiences and applications — practical implementations.
7. Evidence — research, telemetry, outcomes, failures and learning.

## 12. Canonical statement

> The Organisational Evolution Theory explains why organisations evolve. The Organisational Evolution Framework explains how that evolution unfolds. The Organisational Evolution Canon preserves and improves what humanity learns while making the journey.
